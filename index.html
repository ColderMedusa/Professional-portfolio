Intro

Hi, I'm Jerdam — a data engineer with a First Class BEng in Mechanical Engineering and around 18 months of commercial data engineering experience. I build the pipelines, infrastructure, and systems that move data from where it's generated to where it's useful.

My background bridges two worlds: mechanical design and data engineering. I've designed and simulated gear systems in SolidWorks for FEA analysis, built semi-autonomous vehicles with Arduino-based control, and more recently been working across Python, SQL, PostgreSQL, Kafka, Spark, and Airflow on production data pipelines. I'm interested in what happens when those two sides meet — particularly in cyber-physical systems AI, predictive maintenance, and robotics.

This portfolio is where I share what I've worked on, what I'm learning, and where I'm heading. Thanks for stopping by.

About

I'm Jerdam Chukwu — a graduate mechanical engineer with a First Class BEng from the University of East London, and a data engineer with commercial experience across Python, SQL, PostgreSQL, Kafka, Spark, Airflow, Azure, AWS, Databricks, Terraform, Docker, and Power BI.

My path into data engineering grew out of mechanical engineering — from designing and simulating harmonic gears in FEA, to building a semi-autonomous vehicle with Arduino-based control, to exploring data-driven engineering solutions with Python. That background is what shapes my interest in cyber-physical systems AI: the intersection of mechanical engineering, data engineering, and artificial intelligence.

Right now my focus is on predictive maintenance for autonomous systems and adaptive robotic fabrication — using real-time sensor data to drive decisions in physical systems. Long-term, I want to work at this interface: in academic research, industrial R&D, or applied research leadership.

When I'm not building pipelines or reading about robotics, I'm usually working on a postgraduate application or a side project. Always happy to connect with people doing interesting work in data, engineering, or AI.
